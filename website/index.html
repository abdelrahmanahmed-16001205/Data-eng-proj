HOW TO READ THIS STORY

A geographic association, not a cause

The project links housing records and meter locations using a square-grid proximity window because the datasets do not share a direct identifier. Results can identify patterns for further study, but cannot show that meters cause housing development or that housing changes meter availability.

Limits of the data

The housing file is a current dataset of project/building records; a row is not necessarily a unique physical building. Meter locations and status are a current snapshot, not historical records for each completion date. A meter is not the same as a parking space, and the grid is not walking distance. Missing coordinates cannot be assigned a reliable proximity count.

THE DATA STORY

Two city systems, one map

In the notebook's recorded run, 75.7% of housing records with usable coordinates had at least one active meter in the selected grid window; 77.2% of meter records with usable coordinates had a housing record nearby. The overlap varied by borough and by the size of the proximity window, so these figures describe the chosen method and data snapshot.

Why it matters: planners and housing advocates can use this as an initial screening view to identify neighborhoods for closer study. Before making policy decisions, they would need historical meter data, parking occupancy, transit access, and a more direct measure of access.
